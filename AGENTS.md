# BarangAI project rules

- Follow the user's global AGENTS.md instructions.
- Build a barangay incident-reporting app. Since 3 Nov 2026 this is a standalone project, no longer tied to the PLDT AI in 30 Hours hackathon or the DLSU-D paper, so features may go beyond the paper. Each staff account belongs to one barangay; administrators see all. Staff and administrators use the same dashboard, differing only in that limit; staff decide deletion requests for their own barangay. Never auto-forward reports to outside offices.
- Required roles: resident, staff, administrator. Enforce permissions in the API.
- Keep model prediction, resident category, and staff-verified category distinct.
- Categories: flooding, fallen_trees, broken_streetlights, exposed_wiring, potholes, uncollected_garbage, damaged_signage, other. The model predicts only its trained subset.
- Required report path: Pending, Under Verification, Accepted, In Progress, Resolved; Under Verification may become Rejected, with a required reason from a fixed list. Every status change by staff or administrators needs a description, and residents see both; on a grouped case, every resident in it does, and "Already reported" is refused because rejection moves the whole case (3 Nov 2026). Residents see status history and staff `action` notes only: no staff IDs, coordination notes or automatic logs. AI hazard flags stay off the shared map (3 Nov 2026).
- Store private photographs outside public web roots. Strip metadata before storage.
- Use synthetic people and incidents in demos and tests. Never invent model metrics or user-study results.
- Keep external services optional in local tests. No paid API use or automatic billing.
- Do not commit, push, or deploy without the user's direct request or existing authorization in this chat. Commits are authored as the team member; commit messages, branch names and pull requests never name the tools used to write the code (no co-author or session lines).

## Decisions (26 Oct 2026)

- Points and rewards (4 Nov 2026): residents earn points when staff accept a report (25) and when it is resolved (10), with 5 extra for the first reporter of a group, at most 5 rewarded reports a day and once per incident. 5 points = ₱1; amounts are starting values, set in the environment. One balance spends in any barangay's shop, for a single-use code that expires in 30 days with a refund. Staff manage and honour their own barangay's rewards; administrators all. Deleting a report takes back its points, never below zero. The app moves no money.
- Hosting (changed 3 Nov 2026): runs locally. The laptop serves the site, API, SQLite database and private photos (`deploy/start-local.ps1 -Lan`); staff use its browser and residents use an Android app over the same Wi-Fi. The app is a Capacitor shell (`apps/mobile`) that loads the site from the laptop, keeping one origin and cookie auth, with native GPS because browsers refuse it on plain HTTP. Not a phone-only rewrite: reports must be shared between residents and staff. Render, Supabase and their files are parked, not in use. Live updates are polling, not push: while on screen the app checks notices every 5 seconds and reports every 15, chosen over WebSockets or server-sent events because it needs no new server parts (4 Nov 2026).
- Auth: keep the app's own sessions (argon2, cookie + CSRF, Google OAuth). Not Supabase Auth. No email service: abuse is limited by database-counted caps (`usage.py`), and forgotten passwords are reset from the command line (3 Nov 2026).
- Frontend: Vite React SPA in feature folders, React Router, TanStack Query, Tailwind + shadcn/ui (guided by the `ui-ux-pro-max` skill). Not Next.js. Duolingo-inspired look (3 Nov 2026, replacing the Apple-like one): Nunito bundled with the app (no font CDN), white canvas, Eager Green #58cc02 for the primary action, Kap's vest and progress, Spark Blue #1cb0f6 for links and secondary actions, chunky 2.5D pressable buttons and cards with a solid bottom edge (no 3D models), light/dark following the device with an in-app toggle. Residents use phones (bottom tabs, Home first, Android app); staff and admins use desktops (sidebar dashboard), with bottom tabs and a fixed top bar on phones. The desktop website (resident, staff, admin) was redesigned first, then the resident phone screens once the Android app landed (3 Nov 2026). Each destination appears once per screen (one Report action: the green sidebar button on desktops, the raised tab on phones; the staff queue through the Reports link and its badge), labels wrap rather than overflow in Filipino, Filipino copy is short everyday Taglish rather than formal Tagalog (photo, ulat, staff, i-check), task screens (sign-in, report steps, chat) fit one phone screen without scrolling, the Report tab is the same size as the other tabs, and the Android app is portrait-only on phones (Android 16 lets tablets and unfolded foldables rotate) (owner, 4 Nov 2026). UI changes need the user's approval of `visual-check` screenshots.
- Mascot Kap: an owl in a salakot; 2D stills cut from the user's own owl pose sheet (vest recoloured green and its printed label replaced with "KAP" at the owner's request, 3 Nov 2026; otherwise the supplied pixels are only keyed and recoloured) and the owl-head sticker as the app logo (also the Android launcher icon and splash), animated with CSS/Motion; the owner's v2 sticker sheet and three bust sheets (3 Nov 2026) arrive already green with KAP. The sticker poses are the mascot across the whole system; the bust frames are for sign-in only (owner, 4 Nov 2026), cut to whole cells so they swap in place for blinking, eye-tracking and covering his eyes; never redrawn or regenerated. 3D models are shelved (procedural Blender builds were rejected); depth comes from layering, tilt and parallax.
- Chatbot (3 Nov 2026, owner's decision, replacing keyword matching and curated topics): the model reads the resident's message and judges whether it is about the app, answering as Kap or returning a verdict that shows a fixed refusal. Emails, phone numbers and report IDs are masked first on a best-effort basis, report status stays database-only, and the privacy page says Cloudflare receives chat text.
- Motion: playful but quick, after Duolingo (150–300 ms; springy press on buttons, bounce on Kap, the map pin and success moments; staff screens stay calmer), Motion library, CSS where enough, off under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Refactor order: fix, then redesign the frontend, then improve the backend. The old EfficientNet classifier (`inference.py`, `ml/`, `models/`) stays frozen and unused; `duplicates.py` stays frozen and is reused as is. The chatbot is in scope.
- Staff jurisdiction (2 Nov 2026): the server places each report in a barangay from its pin, using GeoJSON boundaries checked in plain Python (no PostGIS). Staff get a 404 outside their barangay; reports outside every boundary are for administrators only; a case stays in one barangay. Enforcement starts when the first barangay is loaded, so all staff share one desk until then. Not Supabase row policies: permissions stay in the API. Coverage (3 Nov 2026): the 75 barangays of Dasmariñas, using PSGC names and the NAMRIA/PSA shapes matched to them (`apps/api/data/dasmarinas-barangays.geojson`), chosen over OpenStreetMap as the more official source. The 13 barangays around DLSU Taft in Manila (`apps/api/data/taft-barangays.geojson`, same source, within about 250 m of the campus reading) were added on 3 Nov 2026 so demos work there too. Pins outside still submit, with a warning, and go to administrators.
- Report AI (option C, 28 Oct 2026): a Cloudflare Workers AI vision model (Mistral Small 3.1 24B; Gemma 3 is not allowed on the Workers Free account) writes the category, a description unique to each photo and hazard flags; MobileCLIP2-S0 (ONNX on Render, research-only licence: on 3 Nov 2026 the owner chose to keep it for now and review the terms) cross-checks the category, stands in when Cloudflare is down and gives duplicate embeddings. Location comes from the photo's GPS, then the phone's, with OpenStreetMap street names. When nothing looks broken, the AI still picks the closest category, marks it "AI unsure · please check" and says so in the description; checker-only and broken-streetlight guesses always ask the resident to check. Auto-fill only assists: residents review and edit all of it, and Review shows a disclaimer and a required tick whenever an AI or GPS value is still in use (1 Nov 2026); on Rokid Glasses the confirm screen shows the disclaimer and the resident's confirm press is that tick, so a report can be sent from the glasses (owner, 4 Nov 2026); staff verify. Not SigLIP: Hugging Face Spaces with CPU are paid on the user's account. No local training data; tune and evaluate only with `apps/api/tools/evaluate_ai.py`, keeping tune and test photos apart. Face blurring uses YuNet (MIT, 3 Nov 2026), not the Haar cascade, which blurred wires and poles and missed tilted and small faces. License plates are blurred with OpenCV's LPD-YuNet (Apache 2.0, 4 Nov 2026), trained on Chinese plates and tuned here on drawn plates only, chosen over YOLOv9 detectors whose licence could not be confirmed.
